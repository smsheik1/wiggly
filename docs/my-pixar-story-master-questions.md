# My Pixar Story — Master Question Vault ("The Lore Sheet")

This is the comprehensive collection of all story-trigger questions designed to extract authentic, visual, emotional "Mom and Dad Lore" for a 3D Pixar-style short film.

Use this sheet to compare, cherry-pick, and trim into the final intake menu.

---

## Chapter 1: Childhood Lore, Mischief & Obsessions (Ages 6–11)
*Pixar Goal: Establish the kid protagonist’s spirit, scale, innocence, and quirky world.*

| # | Question / Prompt | The "Lore" Angle & Emotional Trigger | Pixar Visual Payoff |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1A** | **The Partner-in-Crime & Mischief:**<br> *"Who was your partner-in-crime growing up, and what was the silliest trouble or craziest adventure you two got into?"* | Rekindles memories of treehouses, mud mounds, sneaking out, prank calls, street games, neighborhood rivalries. | Dynamic camera, kids running in golden hour dust, jumping ramps, laughing hysterically. |
| **1B** | **Getting into Trouble:**<br> *"Did you ever get into big trouble as a kid? What happened, and did your parents catch you?"* | Breaks the "stuffy parent" facade immediately. Kids love hearing that their parents were imperfect kids. | A guilty 3D kid with wide eyes standing in front of a broken lamp or muddy hallway. |
| **1C** | **The Weird Childhood Obsession:**<br> *"What was your weird childhood obsession that you were 100% convinced was the coolest thing on the planet?"* | Nostalgic deep dive into Lego empires, dinosaurs, baseball cards, drawing comic strips, rollerblading, catching fireflies. | A chaotic, colorful childhood bedroom stacked with their specific obsessive hobby. |
| **1D** | **The Grand Ambition:**<br> *"What did you genuinely think you were going to be when you grew up, before the real world got in the way?"* | Unfiltered imagination: astronaut, rockstar, marine biologist, inventor, pro athlete. | Kid wearing cardboard helmet and flashlight looking up at a starry night sky. |
| **1E** | **Roots & The Childhood House:**<br> *"Did your family stay put in one neighborhood or move around a lot? What is one sensory memory (a smell, a specific porch or backyard) you can still feel?"* | Sensory grounding of roots (small town vs. suburbs vs. city vs. moving 5 times). | Warm wide shot of the childhood home exterior with seasonal weather (autumn leaves / summer porch). |
| **1F** | **The Secret Childhood Sanctuary:**<br> *"Where was your favorite hiding spot or kingdom where you felt like you owned the world?"* | The treehouse, under the living room blanket fort, the attic, the backyard creek. | Cozy, warmly lit secret clubhouse fort seen from child eye-level. |

## Chapter 2: The Freedom Machine & High School Era (Ages 12–18)
*Pixar Goal: Identity formation, hilarious teenage awkwardness, and the first taste of independence.*

| # | Question / Prompt | The "Lore" Angle & Emotional Trigger | Pixar Visual Payoff |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **2A** | **The "First Taste of Freedom" Machine:**<br> *"What was your first real taste of freedom? What were you driving, riding, or running around in, and where was the first place you bolted to when you got the keys?"* | Instant excitement: the first beat-up 1992 Honda Civic, the BMX bike, the skateboard, cruising main street or diner parking lot. | Cinematic night cruise, wind blowing hair through cracked window, neon diner dashboard glow. |
| **2B** | **The "Secret Identity" Reveal:**<br> *"What is one thing about you as a teenager that would completely shock your kids if they saw you back then?"* | Parents reveal they were garage band rockers, state wrestling champions, neon-haired goths, or breakdancers. | Hilarious, vibrant teenage character model rocking their real retro outfit and hairstyle. |
| **2C** | **The High School Tribe / Persona:**<br> *"What was your crowd or vibe in high school? Were you the jock, the theater kid, the gamer/nerd, the rebel, or the quiet observer?"* | Captures social identity and where they felt they belonged (or didn't belong). | Bustling high school hallway, lockers, letterman jackets, band instruments, or library stacks. |
| **2D** | **The Passion / Talent Sink:**<br> *"What sport, talent, or hobby consumed all your afternoons and weekends back then?"* | The obsession that shaped their discipline: basketball gym, stage rehearsals, coding on a CRT monitor, track field. | Dramatic action shot of the teen in the zone (sweat on forehead, spotlight, gymnasium lights). |
| **2E** | **The Parental Dynamic:**<br> *"How was your relationship with your own parents back then? Were you closer to your mom or your dad, and what was one phrase they drilled into you?"* | Family roots, strict vs. warm home dynamics, generational wisdom and humor. | Kitchen table dinner or garage workshop scene with parents offering tough love or warm support. |

## Chapter 3: The Leap of Faith & The Crucible (Ages 19–25)
*Pixar Goal: The hero leaving the nest, facing the storms of the real world, and discovering resilience.*

| # | Question / Prompt | The "Lore" Angle & Emotional Trigger | Pixar Visual Payoff |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **3A** | **The Leap of Faith:**<br> *"What was the craziest risk or adventure you took when you first stepped out into the world on your own?"* | Leaving hometown with two duffle bags, backpacking, starting an ambitious business, taking a wild gamble. | Character standing in a train station or airport terminal looking out at the big city skyline. |
| **3B** | **The Crucible (The Toughest Mountain):**<br> *"What was the hardest challenge you faced in your twenties where you genuinely had to dig deep and wonder if you'd make it?"* | Scraping rent money, heartbreaking failure, loneliness, tough military/academic grind, unexpected loss. | Quiet, rainy evening, solitary figure at a small desk or moving boxes, determined expression. |
| **3C** | **Finding the Calling:**<br> *"How did you end up picking your career or life's work? Was it a grand plan, pure accident, or necessity turned into passion?"* | The pivot from childhood daydream to adult craft and pride. | The character stepping into their real-world workspace with pride and hard-won competence. |

## Chapter 4: Mom & Dad's Origin Story (Romance)
*Pixar Goal: The romantic collision—two lives intertwining through quirky, memorable human moments.*

| # | Question / Prompt | The "Lore" Angle & Emotional Trigger | Pixar Visual Payoff |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **4A** | **How Did You Meet Mom / Dad?:**<br> *"How did you two first cross paths, and what was your exact first impression when you laid eyes on each other?"* | The classic meet-cute: college hallway, workplace breakroom, blind date, mutual friends, random encounter. | Soft-focus bokeh, first glance across a crowded room, eyes locking. |
| **4B** | **The Awkward Romance / "Trying Too Hard":**<br> *"What was the funniest, most awkward, or dumbest thing you did when you were trying to impress them early on?"* | People love laughing at their uncool younger selves: spilled drinks, getting lost, cheesy pickup lines, failed plans. | Charming, hilarious expressive Pixar character expressions, blushing, clumsy charm. |
| **4C** | **How Did You Know They Were "The One"?:**<br> *"What was the exact, quiet moment when the lightbulb turned on and you thought: 'Yup, this is the person I want to do life with'?"* | Not the Hollywood fireworks, but the moment they got sick, laughed at a dumb joke, or talked in the car till 3 AM. | Warm rain on a car windshield, sharing food at a late-night diner booth, gentle smile. |
| **4D** | **What I Wish My Spouse Understood:**<br> *"Raising a family is a whirlwind. What is one quiet sacrifice or way you show love for your spouse that you feel words don't always capture?"* | Deep marital vulnerability and appreciation that softens hearts. | Gentle, tender look between the couple while handling household or family life. |

## Chapter 5: Parenthood, Truths & Legacy (Present Day)
*Pixar Goal: The emotional payoff—bridging the gap between the parent and their children.*

| # | Question / Prompt | The "Lore" Angle & Emotional Trigger | Pixar Visual Payoff |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **5A** | **The Secret Identity (For the Kids):**<br> *"What is one thing you wish your kids truly understood about who you are inside—the person you were before they were born?"* | Reveals that Dad/Mom had dreams, worries, was once scared and winging it too, just like their kids are. | Adult parent looking down at their child with deep, soulful warmth and empathy. |
| **5B** | **What I Wish the World Understood:**<br> *"What is a part of your character or resilience that you wish people or the world saw more clearly?"* | Dignity, quiet integrity, uncelebrated grit. | Heroic, grounded portrait in warm cinematic natural light. |
| **5C** | **The Core Family Value:**<br> *"Looking back at the whole wild ride, what is the single most important truth or lesson you want your family to carry forever?"* | The family creed: kindness, never giving up, family first, finding humor in everything. | The family gathered together, laughing, authentic cozy togetherness. |
| **5D** | **The Time-Machine Hug (The Grand Finale):**<br> *"If you could step into a time machine right now, walk up to that 8-year-old version of yourself in the backyard, and give them a hug—what would you tell them?"* | Pure Pixar emotional peak (like the ending of *Up* or *Coco*). Tears guaranteed. | Modern 3D adult character kneeling down in front of their 8-year-old child self, smiling with pride. |
